## Decisions

- **The scenarios measure promises, not HTTP.** k6's built-ins time requests; the custom
  trends time what NFR-01, NFR-02 and NFR-05 actually claim — TTFA, sync latency, and how
  fast a refusal arrives.
- **A 429 or 503 is not a failure.** NFR-05 says overload must be refused quickly, so the
  refusal's latency *is* the measurement. Only a response that is neither served nor
  cleanly refused counts as failed.
- **Hit and miss latency are separate trends.** One reads an object, the other runs a
  model; a combined percentile measures neither. Splitting them is what makes a
  threshold on hit latency meaningful.
- **The steady rate is the measured one.** Its "zero rejections" threshold only means
  something if the rate is genuinely at capacity, so capacity was measured first (0.25
  rps on one slot) rather than guessed.

## Two real defects, found by load rather than by reading

- **ADR-007 specifies a bounded queue per class, and there was no bound.** Any number of
  callers could queue and wait the class budget. Implemented as `QueueDepthPerSlot`.
- **The bound alone did not deliver US-12.** Callers admitted into a short queue still
  waited the full 2 s for a refusal they could have had instantly. The dispatcher now
  refuses when the queue ahead of a caller cannot drain inside its class budget, which is
  the literal reading of "never accept-then-timeout".

Rejection latency p95 went from **2 s to 16 ms**. The unit tests passed throughout: they
checked that overload *is refused*, never how long the refusal took. That is the gap a
load test exists to close.
